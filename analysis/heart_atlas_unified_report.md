# Muscle, matrix and vascular development in the postnatal heart

## Abstract

After birth, the heart must grow while becoming capable of sustained pumping. We integrated the P0, P7, P14 and P21 Heart Atlas analyses to examine how cardiomyocyte states, fibroblast environments and vascular development relate across tissue regions. Cardiomyocytes of the same subtype differed in maturation-associated expression within a section. Fibroblasts also expressed changing combinations of matrix genes, and broader assignments recovered late populations missed by strict nuclear-confirmation rules. At P14, approximately 11% of Col8a1 candidates were near arterial structures, compared with 7% of matched Gfpt2 candidates. Early endothelial and CM cell-cycle activity tended to occur together; earlier matched capillary comparisons associated proximity with lower cell-cycle expression at P7 and higher metabolic-regulatory expression at P14/P21. The latest CM-neighbor refit, however, did not establish a general fibroblast effect on maturation: no fibroblast identity or program association passed correction across the full primary test set. A P0 elastic-matrix relationship remains a follow-up candidate. The clearest corrected association was P14 endocardial-like surroundings with Ankrd1-CM sarcomere expression, although it depended on transcript assignment. Updated descriptions of cross-age matches showed a P7-to-P14 shift from Postn-associated toward Col8a1-associated expression, without establishing cell ancestry. Together, the analyses identify specific regional environments and developmental comparisons to test with independent hearts, direct boundary validation and measurements of vascular and CM function.

## Introduction

The heart must meet increasing demands after birth. Its muscle grows, cardiomyocytes change how they use energy, and their contractile machinery develops. These changes take place while blood vessels expand and fibroblasts organize the extracellular matrix around and between cells. Cardiomyocyte development therefore depends on more than changes within the muscle cells themselves.

The Heart Atlas allows us to examine these processes in the same tissue [1]. We ask whether cardiac cell subtypes occupy different locations and relate differently to one another. Ankrd1-associated and working ventricular cardiomyocytes may respond differently to their surroundings. Fibroblasts expressing Postn-, Gfpt2-, Col8a1- or Lsamp-associated patterns may contribute different features of the developing matrix.

The vascular study by Lu and colleagues provides an important anatomical setting for these questions [2]. It showed that existing coronary vessels expand around birth, especially into the inner myocardium—the ventricular muscle toward the chamber cavity and its endocardial lining. We use this finding to ask how growing vessels, changing fibroblast states and cardiomyocyte development might coordinate within different parts of the ventricular wall.

The latest fibroblast reassessment is incorporated throughout this report. We distinguish which fibroblasts occupy a region from whether their identities or shared expression patterns explain differences between neighboring CMs. This brings tissue organization, local associations and developmental comparisons into one account without treating them as equivalent evidence for a mechanism.

## Results

### The major cardiac populations define different tissue settings

The atlas places the major cardiac populations in a common spatial setting: muscle cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, pericytes, smooth-muscle cells, immune cells and epicardial cells (Fig. 1a–d). Their distributions provide the tissue context for more specific subtype comparisons. A concentration of smooth-muscle cells, for example, helps identify a vascular setting, while surface-associated cells and chamber-facing structures define different environments from the surrounding muscle. These broad maps do not establish communication between neighboring cells.

We retain five practical endothelial categories: capillary-like, endocardial-like, arterial-like, venous-like and lymphatic-like (Fig. 1e–h). The maps show the supported subset, with unassigned endothelial profiles kept visible. A sparse subtype map does not prove that the corresponding vessels are absent. Capillary and endocardial environments are examined separately because a cell lining the chamber and a cell belonging to the coronary network have different anatomical roles.

Cycling or sprouting-associated expression describes an endothelial state that can occur within a vascular category. It should not be treated as a separate vessel identity or as proof that a vessel is delivering blood. Similarly, a lymphatic assignment requires endothelial identity and a broader lymphatic marker pattern; Lyve1 alone is insufficient.

### A cardiomyocyte subtype does not have one fixed level of maturity

At P0 and P7, both Ankrd1-associated and working ventricular cardiomyocytes showed a wide range of maturation-associated gene expression within the same section (Fig. 2). Two cardiomyocytes with the same subtype label could therefore differ in their expression of genes involved in energy metabolism and contraction. Subtype identity and developmental state describe different features of a cell.

We measured these differences using a panel of 21 genes and examined the metabolic, structural and cell-cycle patterns separately. A higher panel score means stronger expression of the measured maturation-associated genes. It does not tell us directly how strongly a cell contracts or how efficiently it produces energy. Separating these measurements matters because a cardiomyocyte can retain cell-cycle activity while developing other aspects of its function.

The subtypes also occupied different parts of the tissue. Slit2-associated cardiomyocytes were more often found toward the chamber lining in the released maps. Ankrd1 expression was enriched near identified arterial structures, particularly at P7 and P14, but many Ankrd1-associated cardiomyocytes were elsewhere. Because Ankrd1 can accompany adaptation to mechanical loading, this pattern raises the possibility that local forces or matrix organization contribute to the state of these cells. It does not establish an exclusively artery-associated subtype or indicate disease in the developing heart. The native Slit2 and Ankrd1 maps provide additional regional context (Fig. S4). Kcnd2 and Irx5 are not measured in the deposited 403-gene panel, so those genes cannot directly locate a proposed transmural subtype in Xenium.

### Fibroblasts acquire different matrix-associated features

Early fibroblasts prominently expressed a Postn-associated pattern. At later ages, Col8a1-, Gfpt2- and Lsamp-associated groups became more apparent in the RNA data (Fig. 3). Postn expression fell substantially by P21, although some transcripts remained detectable. This early Postn pattern is consistent with normal tissue growth and remodeling; its presence does not imply pathological fibrosis.

The later fibroblast groups differed in the matrix genes they expressed. At P14 and P21, Col8a1-associated fibroblasts showed higher expression of genes associated with basement-matrix organization than Gfpt2 fibroblasts. This difference was seen in both RNA libraries. Lsamp-associated groups included elastic-matrix and collagen-IV-associated patterns. Within the Col8a1 group, Svep1- and Hmcn2-associated subgroups differed in adhesion and matrix-organization genes. These findings suggest that later fibroblasts may contribute different properties to the tissue around muscle cells and vessels. We have not yet measured the matrix proteins they deposit or the mechanical properties that result.

The named P21 RNA groups provide more detail: Col8a1/Svep1, Col8a1/Hmcn2, Lsamp/Slit3 and Lsamp/Col4a5 fibroblasts differ in their matrix-related expression. Those names replace the F-number labels. Their finer positions are not established at the same resolution in Xenium. Similarities between selected early and later RNA groups nominate comparisons for lineage tracing; they do not show that an early fibroblast becomes a particular late subtype.

### Fibroblast identity and shared expression describe different parts of development

The revised maps recover substantial late Col8a1- and Lsamp-associated populations (Fig. 4). At P21, candidate identities are available for 7,992 of 10,867 eligible fibroblast profiles, or about 74%, compared with about 6% under the earlier stringent rules. This broadens coverage; it does not establish greater accuracy. The classifiers' reference thresholds were selected from the same predictions used to assess their apparent precision, so that precision is not an unbiased estimate of future performance.

Some families cannot be assessed at particular ages because they are absent from the same-age reference. We now mark those entries as “not assessable,” rather than presenting them as biological zeros. For example, the lack of a late Postn-family assignment does not mean that every late fibroblast has stopped expressing Postn. It means that the reference does not supply a separate late Postn family for the classifier to recognize.

The directly measured gene patterns provide complementary information (Fig. S1). The combined Postn/Ptn/Tnc/Col24a1 pattern was more prominent early and declined later, while Col8a1- and Gfpt2-associated patterns became more apparent. Individual Postn RNA can persist within later groups even as the broader early pattern weakens. At P21, 192 eligible fibroblast profiles met the descriptive Postn-pattern enrichment rule. This is different from identifying a persistent, distinct early Postn subtype.

Gfpt2 illustrates why both kinds of information matter. Only 85 P21 profiles received the broader candidate Gfpt2 identity, whereas 1,339 showed enrichment of the measured Gfpt2/Pi16/Adgrd1 pattern. Of these, 327 also showed the Col8a1-associated pattern. These are different measurements, not competing counts of one biological population. A fibroblast can resemble one subtype while sharing part of another group's expression pattern.

### A Gfpt2-associated pattern extends beyond a narrow subtype

The reference tests show why choosing the method that assigns the most Gfpt2 cells would be misleading: some methods recover more Gfpt2 reference nuclei while also assigning other fibroblasts incorrectly. We therefore retain the narrow candidate identity and the broader measured Gfpt2-associated pattern separately (Fig. 5).

After the naming genes were hidden from the classifiers, 280 P21 profiles still received a majority Gfpt2 assignment. When the hidden genes were inspected afterward, Gfpt2 was detected in about 84% and Pi16 in about 91% of these profiles. A smaller subset of 75 retained whole-cell, inner-cell and naming-gene support. These numbers describe different evidence levels, not competing estimates of one population.

The RNA reference also shows overlap: Gfpt2 was detected in about two-thirds of nuclei in the Col8a1/Hmcn2 group, whereas the distinct Gfpt2 group more strongly expressed the combined Gfpt2/Pi16/Adgrd1 pattern. Col8a1 also appears among the paper's Lsamp markers. These genes are informative but not exclusive identifiers. Overlap may reflect related states, shared functions or residual RNA mixing; it does not establish a transition.

### Coronary growth changes the environment of the inner ventricular muscle

Lu and colleagues showed that the coronary network expands from existing vessels around birth, with especially strong growth toward the inner ventricular wall [2]. This begins before birth and extends into developing trabecular muscle. The expansion occurs alongside regional hypoxia and growth of the ventricular wall. By P7, vessel density in the inner myocardium approaches that of the outer wall, and the early angiogenic activity appears to decline between P7 and P14 (Fig. 6a).

This sequence suggests that different parts of the ventricular wall experience vascular growth at different times. Some regions may still be expanding their vessel network while others have more established vascular coverage. These differences could help explain why cardiomyocytes within the same section show different developmental states. Vessel coverage alone, however, does not tell us whether those vessels are delivering sufficient blood or oxygen.

The coronary network must also be distinguished from the endocardium, which lines the heart chamber. In the vascular study, cells labeled in the endocardium around birth did not generate the expanding postnatal coronary network. A cardiomyocyte near the chamber lining therefore has a different vascular and mechanical environment from one alongside a coronary capillary. These settings should be examined separately even though both contain endothelial cells.

### Vascular growth and later support accompany different steps in CM development

At P0 and P7, regions with more cell-cycle activity in endothelial cells also tended to show more cell-cycle activity in cardiomyocytes (Fig. 6b). This is consistent with coordinated growth of the muscle and its blood supply. Cardiomyocytes in these regions can still express genes involved in energy metabolism and contraction: cell-cycle activity and maturation are not opposite ends of a single developmental scale.

Cardiomyocytes positioned alongside capillary endothelial cells showed a changing pattern with age. At P7, they showed lower expression of cell-cycle genes. At P14 and P21, they showed higher expression of genes involved in energy metabolism (Fig. 6c). Together, these observations suggest that early vascular growth and later vascular support may accompany different steps in cardiomyocyte development. We have not yet established whether these relationships depend on blood flow, oxygen delivery or signals from neighboring cells.

These earlier matched capillary comparisons use a broader CM cohort than the subtype-specific refit below. They were not rerun for this document. Some effects weakened under stricter capillary definitions, and the data do not establish that a metabolic relationship begins at one particular age. Different detectability across stages is not a demonstrated developmental switch.

### Broader Col8a1 maps retain an arterial-associated component

At P14, Col8a1 candidates were more often near identified arterial structures than matched Gfpt2 candidates (Fig. 9a,b). With the broader assignments, approximately 11% of Col8a1 profiles were within 50 µm of an arterial structure, compared with approximately 7% of matched Gfpt2 profiles. The smaller, crosschecked candidate subset gave a similar direction, approximately 11% versus 6%.

The earlier stringent comparison was approximately 19% versus 6%. Broadening the assignments therefore reduces the contrast while retaining its direction. Most Col8a1 candidates are farther than 50 µm from these structures. The result supports examining a vessel-associated component within a broadly distributed matrix-associated population, rather than treating Col8a1 fibroblasts as an exclusively peri-arterial subtype.

These fibroblasts may help organize matrix around vessels or between vessels and muscle. Their location alone does not establish that role. The matching accounts for RNA depth, cell area, nuclear fraction and local cell density, but does not establish an arterial signal or independent-heart replication. Many Col8a1 profiles were compared with reused Gfpt2 controls, so the number of matched pairs is not the number of independent biological comparisons.

### The local comparisons do not establish a general fibroblast effect on maturation

We recalculated the CM relationships using the broader candidate identities, a more consistently supported subset, the earlier stringent labels and seven measured fibroblast patterns. We examined CM cell-cycle, energy-metabolism, sarcomere and excitation–contraction expression separately, alongside the overall 21-gene score (Fig. S2; full identity results in the source tables).

The main comparisons use CMs of the same subtype within local tissue regions. They account for vascular surroundings, cell composition, RNA capture and cell size. This matters because fibroblasts and CMs may both differ between regions without one directly instructing the other.

Across 925 primary comparisons, no fibroblast subtype or program association passed the stated multiple-testing correction. This does not show that fibroblasts have no developmental role. It means that these sections and this measured panel do not establish a reproducible local maturation effect of one named fibroblast population. Some patterns remain useful hypotheses, but their strength depends on CM identity, the RNA component measured and the definition of the neighborhood.

Several relationships also changed after anatomical adjustment or when comparisons were restricted to local regions. There was no single rule that fibroblast environments consistently raised or lowered all aspects of CM maturation. The developing matrix is therefore better viewed as a set of regional environments to investigate than as one uniformly promoting or inhibitory influence.

### The earlier P7 Postn relationship weakens with broader assignments

In the earlier stringent analysis, cycling-associated CMs near Postn fibroblasts showed lower expression of genes involved in energy metabolism. The negative estimate became smaller and uncertain when we used the broader candidate identities or the crosschecked subset (Fig. 7a). This weakens the argument that a particular Postn fibroblast subtype suppresses CM metabolic development.

Examining the measured Postn-associated pattern gave negative estimates in several RNA views (Fig. 7b). However, when the same cycling-associated CMs were compared using a smaller, 30-µm neighborhood, the relationship was close to zero and uncertain (Fig. 7d). Shared-boundary comparisons also failed to provide consistent support. The observation remains a tentative association with a broader developmental environment, rather than evidence for a direct local effect.

The Gfpt2-associated pattern showed a possible contrasting relationship: at P7, cycling-associated CMs in Gfpt2-pattern-rich surroundings tended to show higher metabolic expression (Fig. 7c). This became less convincing when other fibroblast programs were considered together, and it did not pass correction across the primary comparisons. It suggests a comparison worth testing under controlled conditions, not evidence that Gfpt2 fibroblasts accelerate maturation.

### A P0 elastic-matrix setting provides a specific follow-up hypothesis

At P0, working ventricular CMs surrounded by fibroblasts with a stronger Eln/Fbln1/Fbln2 pattern showed lower sarcomere-gene expression (Fig. 8c). The direction persisted when fibroblast RNA was measured in the inner part of the cell, in the deposited counts and after random RNA loss to a comparable depth. It also persisted in the smaller-neighborhood comparison using the same CMs, when other measured fibroblast programs were included together, and when CM genes were removed from the fibroblast scoring background.

This was the strongest fibroblast-program candidate in the primary analysis, but it did not pass correction across the full test set. The finding could reflect a local matrix environment accompanying structural development, or a regional feature that our anatomical proxies do not fully capture. The measured genes do not establish elastin deposition, tissue stiffness or an outflow-tract location.

A separate P21 pattern linked basement-matrix-associated fibroblast expression with higher sarcomere expression in Ankrd1 CMs (Fig. 8d). That estimate weakened when fibroblast RNA was restricted to the inner-cell region. It is a lower-confidence candidate and should first be checked with direct imaging of the relevant cell boundaries and matrix proteins.

### The clearest corrected relationship concerns the endocardial environment of Ankrd1 CMs

At P14, Ankrd1-associated CMs near endocardial-like endothelial cells showed higher sarcomere-gene expression after adjustment for the new fibroblast identities (Fig. 8a). The relationship also remained when fibroblast gene programs were used instead. It was much smaller in working ventricular CMs, and the difference between the two CM groups was supported in the within-section comparison.

Removing Myl2 from the sarcomere measurement retained the positive relationship in Ankrd1 CMs. The overall 21-gene maturation score did not show a corresponding increase. The observation therefore concerns structural gene expression in a particular CM state, rather than greater overall maturity.

There is an important boundary-related qualification. The relationship was clear using CM RNA from the inner-cell region, but was uncertain using the deposited whole-cell CM counts (Fig. 8b). Smaller-neighborhood comparisons retained a positive direction when the same CMs were examined. Shared-boundary data did not provide an adequately supported corresponding estimate. This makes the result a priority for anatomical and RNA-imaging validation, rather than proof of direct endothelial contact signaling.

The endocardial lining differs from the coronary network that supplies the muscle. Cavity-facing anatomy, local loading and endothelial signals remain competing explanations. Published vascular growth provides the developmental setting, but it does not establish which of these mechanisms explains the P14 structural pattern.

### Cross-age comparisons identify changing surroundings, not tracked transitions

We updated the fibroblast descriptions of the 96 previously selected neighborhoods while retaining the original exact-transport and Wormhole matches. The new fibroblast programs were not used to choose those matches.

Between P7 and P14, matched neighborhoods showed lower Postn-associated and higher Col8a1-associated expression in each of the four examined combinations of CM identity and distance from the chamber lining (Fig. 9c). This provides a consistent descriptive comparison to follow: similar muscle-associated environments can be surrounded by different fibroblast expression patterns at successive ages.

The sampling limits finer interpretation. Fifteen neighborhoods contained no eligible fibroblast among the sampled cells, and the median was only two fibroblasts per neighborhood (Fig. 9d). These comparisons cannot resolve detailed fibroblast transitions. They nominate tissue settings for denser sampling and intermediate-stage validation; neither optimal transport nor Wormhole follows an individual cell or establishes its descendants.

The original calibration explains how much confidence to place in fine matches (Fig. S3). Wormhole's agreement with exact neighborhood distances varies, and the closest matches are less stable. Some exact matches also preserve relative CM maturation ordering—for example, an Ankrd1 comparison farther from the chamber lining between P7 and P14—but this is not consistent across all regions. The useful output is a set of developmental environments to sample more closely, rather than a reconstructed cell history.

## Discussion

The combined findings suggest that muscle, matrix and vessels develop together, while different components of CM development relate differently to their surroundings. Early EC and CM cell-cycle activity can occur together. Later capillary proximity accompanies selected metabolic-regulatory patterns, while the endocardial relationship concerns structural expression in a particular CM state. None of these observations places every cell on a single scale from proliferating to mature.

Published coronary expansion gives these findings an anatomical setting. The inner ventricular muscle undergoes pronounced vascular growth around birth. A useful experimental question is whether improved blood delivery precedes changes in CM energy use, whether the matrix explains differences between regions with similar vascular access, or whether local endothelial contact contributes independently. Regional anatomy must be tested first because neighboring cells can share a developmental environment without one directly instructing the other.

The fibroblast refit narrows the claims we can make. Broader assignments retain a Col8a1-associated component near arteries while reducing the apparent contrast with Gfpt2 cells. They also weaken the earlier P7 Postn–CM metabolic relationship. Shared patterns cross subtype boundaries, so future comparisons should retain both the cells' proposed identities and the programs they express. The current evidence does not establish one fibroblast subtype as a general promoter or suppressor of CM maturation.

Two structural settings deserve focused validation. At P0, working CMs in Eln/Fbln1/Fbln2-associated surroundings show lower sarcomere expression across several sensitivity checks, although the candidate does not pass the full primary correction. At P14, Ankrd1 CMs near endocardial-like cells show higher sarcomere expression in core RNA, with a weaker relationship in deposited whole-cell counts. Clear membrane outlines, same-cell RNA/protein measurements and matched ventricular anatomy should precede a mechanistic claim about either setting.

Experiments can then separate the competing explanations. Perfusion and local oxygen measurements would test the blood-supply hypothesis. Defined fibroblast or matrix environments under comparable oxygen conditions would test matrix contributions. Altering endothelial contact or local signals while holding oxygen and matrix conditions similar would test contact-related effects. CM measurements should include sarcomere organization, calcium handling, contraction and energy use, with completed division assessed separately from cell-cycle RNA [4,5].

Independent hearts at P3/P5 and around P10 would help establish the order of events. Denser spatial sampling would also strengthen the cross-age comparison, whose sampled neighborhoods currently contain a median of only two eligible fibroblasts. RNA resemblance and optimal-transport correspondence can nominate where to look; lineage tracing is needed to establish which cells contribute to later populations.

One available spatial section per age, incomplete marker resolution and uncertain transcript ownership remain important limits. The integrated story is therefore a set of observed spatial patterns and increasingly specific biological hypotheses. Its next step is direct anatomical and functional validation, followed by a causal experiment chosen from those results.

## Methods

### Data, inherited QC and scope

We used the deposited P0, P7, P14 and P21 snRNA-seq/Xenium data [1] and the frozen reviewed annotations, coordinates, CM cohorts and spatial graphs. There is one available spatial section per age. The latest incorporated analysis fitted CM-neighbor models with the revised fibroblast assignments and directly measured programs. This unified document adds no new biological model or replicate. It does not perform new segmentation, classifier training or spatial imputation.

Inherited snRNA-seq QC used per-library scDblFinder as the primary doublet screen; Scrublet was diagnostic. DecontX was fitted separately to singlet nuclei in eight libraries using called-nucleus clusters without empty droplets. Corrected counts informed the reviewed reference, with raw-reference sensitivity retained. Five residual CM/mural-program fibroblast groups were excluded from the fine reference. Earlier broad-fibroblast median estimated contamination fractions ranged approximately 0.05–0.12 across libraries; these are model estimates, not measured residual contamination after correction. Residual mixing remains possible.

The deposited Xenium matrices contain 403 gene targets plus 138 control/non-gene features. The shared RNA reference contains 381 measured genes. Controls do not enter biological scores. Missing nuclear area alone does not exclude a CM. The earlier multimodal segmentation, transcript reassignment and core/nuclear count layers are retained as sensitivity views, not independent ground truth.

### Fibroblast identities and marker patterns

Identity layers are the newer recommended candidates, the subset supported by cell/core and naming-marker-withheld agreement, and the earlier stringent labels. Broad fibroblast eligibility is frozen. Supported nuclear alternatives remain review flags in the newer labels. Reference classes absent at an age are marked not assessable in the table; their programs can still be measured directly. Candidate counts measure operational coverage rather than tissue abundance. Thresholds selected from reference cross-validation predictions do not provide unbiased prospective spatial accuracy.

Seven measured patterns are analyzed: Postn, Gfpt2, Col8a1 and Lsamp marker sets, plus fibrillar, basement and elastic matrix sets. Exact genes are supplied in the program table and source files. Scores are mean within-cell gene ranks minus the average of 200 control sets matched to pooled clean RNA-reference abundance deciles. The four identity-marker scores reproduce the previous module files. A descriptive enrichment flag exceeds 90% of control sets and requires two detected markers; it is not a cell-type probability or single-cell significance test.

Each source view requires at least 20 shared-panel molecules and eight detected genes. Programs are averaged across all informative eligible fibroblast neighbors, including cells without a confident subtype or enrichment flag. A program is tested only if at least 30 informative fibroblasts detect two of its markers. Identity fractions use all eligible broad fibroblast neighbors, including unassigned cells. Fibroblast program genes do not overlap the CM maturation panel. A separate sensitivity removes CM maturation, lineage/response, cycling and sex genes from both the fibroblast rank background and control pool, then recomputes scores and fits; these excluded genes and retained counts are supplied.

Refined whole-cell, inner-cell core and native deposited counts are compared. Three binomial-thinning repetitions reduce refined counts toward each profile's core depth without changing its geometry; their mean score provides an information-loss control. Core and depth-matched comparisons use the same informative-source mask. These tests do not fully propagate boundary uncertainty.

### Cardiomyocyte outcomes and cohorts

The frozen ventricular cohort retains ventricular-marker-supported profiles with compatible released v.CM, Ankrd1.CM, Slit2.CM, Ddc.CM or p.CM labels and the earlier morphology rule. CMs require at least 20 core-panel molecules and 20 molecules outside the outcome genes. The p.CM label denotes a cycling-associated state, not demonstrated completed cell division.

The measured 21-gene panel is Rxrg, Ppargc1a, Ppargc1b, Cebpa, Cebpb, Esrra, Esrrb, Esrrg, Mitf, Myh6, Tnni3, Myl2, Kcnq1, Jph1, Jph2, Bin1, Lpin1, Pln, Ppara, Ppard and Cpt1b. The frozen direct index is the mean gene-standardized log-normalized expression using the earlier pooled calibration. It is not an exact reproduction of the author's unrecovered AddModuleScore object.

Metabolic-regulatory genes are Ppargc1a, Ppargc1b, Esrra, Esrrg, Ppara, Ppard, Cpt1b and Lpin1. Sarcomere genes are Myh6, Tnni3 and Myl2. Excitation–contraction genes are Pln, Jph1, Jph2, Bin1 and Kcnq1. Cycling uses Mki67, Top2a and Cenpf, with higher values indicating higher cycling-associated RNA. Thus Lpin1 and Pln are grouped by their biological roles rather than reproducing the figure's category placement. Removing Myl2 yields a 20-gene index and a two-gene sarcomere sensitivity without changing ventricular selection. Native CM outcomes provide an additional transcript-ownership sensitivity.

Within each graph, source views use the same eligible CMs. The 50-µm cohorts contain 13,071 P0, 27,659 P7, 26,996 P14 and 27,053 P21 CMs before individual-state model eligibility. Paired 50-/30-µm and shared-boundary comparisons use their common CM cohorts. A fitted state requires at least 200 CMs, eight 400-µm blocks and sufficient residual exposure variation. Categorical exposures also require 50 positive CMs.

### Association models and uncertainty

Primary fibroblast models test one identity fraction or program at a time. They adjust for CM capture outside the outcomes, area, nuclear missingness, neighbor counts, broad non-CM composition and FB/EC capture. Program models additionally adjust for informative-source coverage, depth and detected genes. Anatomical models add arterial/endocardial/large-void/outer-surface distance proxies, morphology class and nuclear-supported capillary/endocardial fractions. The primary comparison removes means within 200-µm regions, separately by CM state. These proxies are not independently reviewed LV/RV or compact/trabecular masks.

Targets and outcomes are standardized within each age/run before separating CM states. Rank-revealing QR removes redundant nuisance columns. Each point estimate is fitted by least squares. Shared 512-resample, 400-µm block bootstraps provide exploratory within-section intervals and CM-subtype slope comparisons. Technical, anatomical and within-region fits are retained separately. Joint-program fits examine specificity; single-program estimates are not assumed independent of other matrix patterns.

Vascular-target models adjust for the revised fibroblast fractions; a sensitivity replaces these fractions with all seven fibroblast programs. The primary family comprises 925 tests across all four ages, eligible CM states, candidate identities, refined programs, two vascular targets and five primary outcomes. One global BH correction is applied to this complete grid. Sensitivity outcomes and alternative definitions are supplied separately and are not used to declare additional corrected discoveries. Different detectability across ages is not treated as a demonstrated age interaction.

### Fibroblast spatial comparisons and temporal descriptions

The P7 Gfpt2/Postn and P14 Col8a1/Gfpt2 comparisons repeat the earlier matching design with the new labels. Profiles need at least five resolved neighbors within 30 µm. Matching uses standardized log RNA molecules, log area, log 50-µm density and nuclear fraction; nearest reference matching permits replacement with a Euclidean caliper of two. Pair IDs, unique reference counts and covariate balance are supplied. Arterial distance is to the earlier operational structure, not a newly traced histological vessel wall. These are descriptive spatial contrasts.

The 96 frozen neighborhoods retain their selected cells, molecular representation, exact transport plans and saved Wormhole plans [3]. Updated fibroblast identities and programs are held-out descriptors. They may share underlying RNA information with the molecular representation, so this is not independent validation. Reported program changes condition on pairs with an informative fibroblast at both ends, and the supported matching mass is supplied. Fine labels do not retrain the encoder. Matching is not ancestry, expansion or a measured transition rate.

### Validation and limitations

Independent checks reconstruct 8,064 neighborhood program means, compare 36 regression coefficients with a separate residualization calculation, verify assignment coverage and unavailable classes, recalculate the full primary BH correction and check design conditioning. Source IDs, source tables, count views and program definitions are retained. Source/artifact hashes and document/figure checks accompany the release.

The atlas's strict outline benchmark deteriorates at P21; its object-overlap score is not transcript-ownership accuracy. Core/native/thinned and shared-boundary checks expose some sensitivities but do not integrate uncertainty over plausible alternative boundaries. Neither computational agreement nor spatial-block intervals establish independent-heart replication. Blood flow, matrix mechanics, functional CM maturation and cell ancestry were not measured here.

### Reference classification and finer RNA states

The clean fibroblast RNA reference contains 14,328 nuclei, with 35 unresolved profiles excluded from named-family training. Three classifiers use the shared panel: balanced multinomial logistic regression, within-cell rank centroids and a negative-binomial count model. Training excludes CM maturation/response, lineage, cycling and sex genes. Opposite-library prediction, naming-gene withholding, raw-reference and pooled late-reference comparisons test sensitivity to the RNA definition. They do not establish independent Xenium truth.

Candidate labels require agreement between at least two reference-screened classifiers and adequate RNA information. Supported core alternatives trigger review. The crosschecked subset also retains core agreement and the same majority assignment after naming genes are hidden. Supported nuclear alternatives remain flags. Reference score screens target apparent precision of at least 0.8 with at least 20 predictions and both libraries represented; thresholds are selected on those same predictions. Full parameters and per-view evidence remain in the alternative-identity release.

Full-RNA matrix comparisons use library-by-family pseudobulks, independently normalized raw/corrected views and summed duplicate gene symbols. Program scores are means of gene log1p CPM. P21 subgroup names retain the reviewed Col8a1/Svep1, Col8a1/Hmcn2, Lsamp/Slit3 and Lsamp/Col4a5 hierarchy. Their finer spatial positions remain unverified. Selected early/late RNA correlations with interpreted markers withheld are candidate similarities, not transition rates.

### Broad cardiac and endothelial maps

Broad cardiac identities and nuclear-supported EC categories retain the earlier atlas/source-QC definitions. The display can subsample dense broad-cell backgrounds, but all calculations retain the recorded cohorts. Endothelial maps show supported capillary-, endocardial-, arterial-, venous- and lymphatic-like sources, with unassigned EC profiles retained in gray. These categories and activity programs do not establish perfusion or lineage fate. Relative CM low/high groups use within-age, within-state ranks, rather than a shared physiological maturity scale.

### Earlier vascular comparisons and transport calibration

The earlier EC/CM cycling analysis uses nuclear-QC sources within 50 µm, with a 30–50-µm shell sensitivity. It measures coordinated RNA expression rather than completed proliferation. The earlier capillary comparison matches CMs within the same 200-µm region and CM state, controlling for capture, area, perimeter and nearby capillary area. It uses a broader cohort than the new subtype-specific models. These earlier estimates and their own uncertainty definitions are retained; they are not added to the 925-test family or refitted in this document.

The 96 transport neighborhoods include 12 per age and CM identity, with four within 100 µm of the endocardial proxy and eight farther away. Up to 32 observed non-CM profiles contribute 16 molecular PCs and six lineage features. Exact distances and saved Wormhole distances are restricted to the same CM identity/distance category. CM maturation is withheld from matching and examined afterward. Approximation and nearest-match agreement remain separate from the updated fibroblast descriptions.

### Original segmentation benchmark

The atlas combines nuclear, membrane and cell-interior staining. Automatic outlines were compared with expert outlines in five P0 and five P21 fields, using TP/(TP+FP+FN) at a demanding 90% overlap threshold. Approximate visual scores are 45–50% at P0 and around 10% at P21; raw values were not recovered. These object scores do not measure the fraction of correctly assigned transcripts. Field counts do not establish independent-heart replication, and relevant overlap/software details remain incompletely specified. A section through a large CM can legitimately lack a visible nucleus. The report therefore retains whole-cell, inner-cell and nuclear evidence without treating one view as ground truth.
