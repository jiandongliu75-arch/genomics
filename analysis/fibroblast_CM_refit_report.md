# Changing fibroblast environments and cardiomyocyte development after birth

## Abstract

Fibroblasts help organize the tissue in which cardiomyocytes grow, but their changing locations do not by themselves establish how they influence muscle development. We reassessed this question in the P0, P7, P14 and P21 Heart Atlas using the revised fibroblast maps and directly measured gene patterns. Early Postn-associated expression declined, while Col8a1- and Gfpt2-associated patterns became more apparent later. These changes crossed subtype boundaries: individual genes could remain detectable after the corresponding early subtype was no longer represented in the same-age reference. At P14, broader Col8a1 candidates were more often near arterial structures than matched Gfpt2 candidates, although the contrast was smaller than in the earlier stringent subset. We then compared cardiomyocytes of the same subtype within local regions, accounting for vascular surroundings and RNA capture. No fibroblast subtype or program association passed correction across the full primary test set. The earlier P7 Postn–CM metabolic relationship weakened with broader assignments. A P0 elastic-matrix relationship remained a candidate for follow-up. The clearest corrected association was higher sarcomere-gene expression in P14 Ankrd1-associated cardiomyocytes near endocardial-like endothelial cells, although it depended on how CM RNA was assigned. Cross-age neighborhood matches also showed a P7-to-P14 shift from Postn-associated toward Col8a1-associated expression. Together, these results identify changing fibroblast environments and specific developmental comparisons to test, while separating tissue organization from evidence of local instruction or cell ancestry.

## Introduction

After birth, cardiomyocytes grow and develop the machinery needed for sustained pumping. Their surroundings are changing at the same time. Coronary vessels expand, the ventricular wall is remodeled, and fibroblasts organize the extracellular matrix around muscle cells and vessels. These processes may support one another, but they need not progress at the same rate in every part of the heart.

The perinatal vascular study by Lu and colleagues provides a useful anatomical framework [2]. Existing coronary vessels expand particularly strongly into the inner ventricular muscle, toward the chamber cavity and its endocardial lining. By P7, vascular density there approaches that of the outer wall. This creates a specific question: as the blood supply expands, do different fibroblast environments accompany different steps in cardiomyocyte development?

Our earlier analysis identified candidate relationships but left an important task unfinished. Many late fibroblasts lacked a subtype under strict nuclear-confirmation rules, and the newer assignments had not been incorporated into the CM comparisons. Here, we complete that refit. We examine both the subtype a fibroblast resembles and the gene patterns it expresses, then ask whether either explains CM differences beyond shared anatomy and vascular proximity.

## Results

### Fibroblast identity and shared expression describe different parts of development

The revised maps recover substantial late Col8a1- and Lsamp-associated populations (Fig. 1). At P21, candidate identities are available for 7,992 of 10,867 eligible fibroblast profiles, or about 74%, compared with about 6% under the earlier stringent rules. This broadens coverage; it does not establish greater accuracy. The classifiers' reference thresholds were selected from the same predictions used to assess their apparent precision, so that precision is not an unbiased estimate of future performance.

Some families cannot be assessed at particular ages because they are absent from the same-age reference. We now mark those entries as “not assessable,” rather than presenting them as biological zeros. For example, the lack of a late Postn-family assignment does not mean that every late fibroblast has stopped expressing Postn. It means that the reference does not supply a separate late Postn family for the classifier to recognize.

The directly measured gene patterns provide complementary information (Fig. 2). The combined Postn/Ptn/Tnc/Col24a1 pattern was more prominent early and declined later, while Col8a1- and Gfpt2-associated patterns became more apparent. Individual Postn RNA can persist within later groups even as the broader early pattern weakens. At P21, 192 eligible fibroblast profiles met the descriptive Postn-pattern enrichment rule. This is different from identifying a persistent, distinct early Postn subtype.

Gfpt2 illustrates why both kinds of information matter. Only 85 P21 profiles received the broader candidate Gfpt2 identity, whereas 1,339 showed enrichment of the measured Gfpt2/Pi16/Adgrd1 pattern. Of these, 327 also showed the Col8a1-associated pattern. These are different measurements, not competing counts of one biological population. A fibroblast can resemble one subtype while sharing part of another group's expression pattern.

### Broader Col8a1 maps retain an arterial-associated component

At P14, Col8a1 candidates were more often near identified arterial structures than matched Gfpt2 candidates (Fig. 6a,b). With the broader assignments, approximately 11% of Col8a1 profiles were within 50 µm of an arterial structure, compared with approximately 7% of matched Gfpt2 profiles. The smaller, crosschecked candidate subset gave a similar direction, approximately 11% versus 6%.

The earlier stringent comparison was approximately 19% versus 6%. Broadening the assignments therefore reduces the contrast while retaining its direction. Most Col8a1 candidates are farther than 50 µm from these structures. The result supports examining a vessel-associated component within a broadly distributed matrix-associated population, rather than treating Col8a1 fibroblasts as an exclusively peri-arterial subtype.

These fibroblasts may help organize matrix around vessels or between vessels and muscle. Their location alone does not establish that role. The matching accounts for RNA depth, cell area, nuclear fraction and local cell density, but does not establish an arterial signal or independent-heart replication. Many Col8a1 profiles were compared with reused Gfpt2 controls, so the number of matched pairs is not the number of independent biological comparisons.

### The local CM comparisons do not establish a general fibroblast effect on maturation

We recalculated the CM relationships using the broader candidate identities, a more consistently supported subset, the earlier stringent labels and seven measured fibroblast patterns. We examined CM cell-cycle, energy-metabolism, sarcomere and excitation–contraction expression separately, alongside the overall 21-gene score (Fig. 3 and Fig. S1).

The main comparisons use CMs of the same subtype within local tissue regions. They account for vascular surroundings, cell composition, RNA capture and cell size. This matters because fibroblasts and CMs may both differ between regions without one directly instructing the other.

Across 925 primary comparisons, no fibroblast subtype or program association passed the stated multiple-testing correction. This does not show that fibroblasts have no developmental role. It means that these sections and this measured panel do not establish a reproducible local maturation effect of one named fibroblast population. Some patterns remain useful hypotheses, but their strength depends on CM identity, the RNA component measured and the definition of the neighborhood.

Several relationships also changed after anatomical adjustment or when comparisons were restricted to local regions. There was no single rule that fibroblast environments consistently raised or lowered all aspects of CM maturation. The developing matrix is therefore better viewed as a set of regional environments to investigate than as one uniformly promoting or inhibitory influence.

### The earlier P7 Postn relationship weakens with broader assignments

In the earlier stringent analysis, cycling-associated CMs near Postn fibroblasts showed lower expression of genes involved in energy metabolism. The negative estimate became smaller and uncertain when we used the broader candidate identities or the crosschecked subset (Fig. 4a). This weakens the argument that a particular Postn fibroblast subtype suppresses CM metabolic development.

Examining the measured Postn-associated pattern gave negative estimates in several RNA views (Fig. 4b). However, when the same cycling-associated CMs were compared using a smaller, 30-µm neighborhood, the relationship was close to zero and uncertain (Fig. 4d). Shared-boundary comparisons also failed to provide consistent support. The observation remains a tentative association with a broader developmental environment, rather than evidence for a direct local effect.

The Gfpt2-associated pattern showed a possible contrasting relationship: at P7, cycling-associated CMs in Gfpt2-pattern-rich surroundings tended to show higher metabolic expression (Fig. 4c). This became less convincing when other fibroblast programs were considered together, and it did not pass correction across the primary comparisons. It suggests a comparison worth testing under controlled conditions, not evidence that Gfpt2 fibroblasts accelerate maturation.

### A P0 elastic-matrix setting provides a specific follow-up hypothesis

At P0, working ventricular CMs surrounded by fibroblasts with a stronger Eln/Fbln1/Fbln2 pattern showed lower sarcomere-gene expression (Fig. 5c). The direction persisted when fibroblast RNA was measured in the inner part of the cell, in the deposited counts and after random RNA loss to a comparable depth. It also persisted in the smaller-neighborhood comparison using the same CMs, when other measured fibroblast programs were included together, and when CM genes were removed from the fibroblast scoring background.

This was the strongest fibroblast-program candidate in the primary analysis, but it did not pass correction across the full test set. The finding could reflect a local matrix environment accompanying structural development, or a regional feature that our anatomical proxies do not fully capture. The measured genes do not establish elastin deposition, tissue stiffness or an outflow-tract location.

A separate P21 pattern linked basement-matrix-associated fibroblast expression with higher sarcomere expression in Ankrd1 CMs (Fig. 5d). That estimate weakened when fibroblast RNA was restricted to the inner-cell region. It is a lower-confidence candidate and should first be checked with direct imaging of the relevant cell boundaries and matrix proteins.

### The clearest corrected relationship concerns the endocardial environment of Ankrd1 CMs

At P14, Ankrd1-associated CMs near endocardial-like endothelial cells showed higher sarcomere-gene expression after adjustment for the new fibroblast identities (Fig. 5a). The relationship also remained when fibroblast gene programs were used instead. It was much smaller in working ventricular CMs, and the difference between the two CM groups was supported in the within-section comparison.

Removing Myl2 from the sarcomere measurement retained the positive relationship in Ankrd1 CMs. The overall 21-gene maturation score did not show a corresponding increase. The observation therefore concerns structural gene expression in a particular CM state, rather than greater overall maturity.

There is an important boundary-related qualification. The relationship was clear using CM RNA from the inner-cell region, but was uncertain using the deposited whole-cell CM counts (Fig. 5b). Smaller-neighborhood comparisons retained a positive direction when the same CMs were examined. Shared-boundary data did not provide an adequately supported corresponding estimate. This makes the result a priority for anatomical and RNA-imaging validation, rather than proof of direct endothelial contact signaling.

The endocardial lining differs from the coronary network that supplies the muscle. Cavity-facing anatomy, local loading and endothelial signals remain competing explanations. Published vascular growth provides the developmental setting, but it does not establish which of these mechanisms explains the P14 structural pattern.

### Cross-age comparisons identify changing surroundings, not tracked cell transitions

We updated the fibroblast descriptions of the 96 previously selected neighborhoods while retaining the original exact-transport and Wormhole matches. The new fibroblast programs were not used to choose those matches.

Between P7 and P14, matched neighborhoods showed lower Postn-associated and higher Col8a1-associated expression in each of the four examined combinations of CM identity and distance from the chamber lining (Fig. 6c). This provides a consistent descriptive comparison to follow: similar muscle-associated environments can be surrounded by different fibroblast expression patterns at successive ages.

The sampling limits finer interpretation. Fifteen neighborhoods contained no eligible fibroblast among the sampled cells, and the median was only two fibroblasts per neighborhood (Fig. 6d). These comparisons cannot resolve detailed fibroblast transitions. They nominate tissue settings for denser sampling and intermediate-stage validation; neither optimal transport nor Wormhole follows an individual cell or establishes its descendants.

## Discussion

The reassessment makes two parts of the story clearer. Fibroblast identities and matrix-associated patterns change across the postnatal sections, and a Col8a1-associated component remains more often near arteries than a matched Gfpt2 component. However, these spatial differences do not translate into one strong, general relationship with neighboring CM maturation. Broader maps improve coverage while also weakening some conclusions drawn from smaller selected subsets.

The P7 Postn result is an example. It remains plausible that an early growth-associated matrix environment accompanies relatively low metabolic expression in cycling-associated CMs. The current data do not establish that Postn fibroblasts cause that state. A controlled comparison should therefore test the matrix environment itself, while keeping starting CM state, oxygen and endothelial support similar.

The P0 elastic-matrix candidate gives a more specific structural question: do Eln/Fbln1/Fbln2-associated surroundings accompany less developed sarcomere organization in working CMs within comparable ventricular regions? The first experiment should combine membrane-resolved RNA imaging, matrix-protein staining and CM structural measurements. A mechanical effect should be proposed only after matrix organization and tissue properties are measured.

The P14 Ankrd1–endocardial relationship remains the clearest corrected association, but its sensitivity to CM transcript assignment changes the immediate priority. We should establish where these CMs lie, confirm sarcomere-marker expression within clearly outlined cells, and compare their structure under similar regional anatomy and loading. Endothelial contact signaling becomes a focused causal question if this association survives those checks.

The vascular findings provide a complementary developmental framework. Earlier analyses found that endothelial and CM cell-cycle activity occurred together at P0/P7, while capillary-adjacent CMs showed lower cell-cycle expression at P7 and higher metabolic-regulatory expression at P14/P21. Those earlier capillary comparisons are retained as context and were not rerun here. They suggest distinct questions about growth and subsequent vascular support, without establishing when a functional blood supply becomes sufficient or whether it drives the RNA changes.

Independent hearts at P3/P5 and around P10 would help order these processes. The most useful measurements would combine vascular perfusion and local oxygen conditions with fibroblast state, matrix organization and CM function in matched ventricular regions. Sarcomere organization, calcium handling, contraction and energy use should be measured directly. Cell-cycle RNA should be distinguished from completed division [4,5].

For cross-age analysis, denser fibroblast sampling should come before claims of fine niche transitions. The current transport comparisons identify a useful P7-to-P14 change in surrounding gene patterns, but their sparse fibroblast representation limits the biological detail. Intermediate stages and lineage tracing are needed to determine whether related states persist, change or contribute to later populations.

The next experiments should therefore be chosen from the observed relationships: validate the P14 structural setting, test the P0 matrix candidate, and examine early metabolic environments under controlled vascular conditions. This provides a practical route from spatial association to developmental mechanism while keeping the unanswered questions explicit.

## Methods

### Data, inherited QC and scope

We used the deposited P0, P7, P14 and P21 snRNA-seq/Xenium data [1] and the frozen reviewed annotations, coordinates, CM cohorts and spatial graphs. There is one available spatial section per age. This analysis fits new CM-neighbor models with the revised fibroblast assignments and directly measured programs. It does not perform new segmentation, classifier training or spatial imputation.

Inherited snRNA-seq QC used per-library scDblFinder as the primary doublet screen; Scrublet was diagnostic. DecontX was fitted separately to singlet nuclei in eight libraries using called-nucleus clusters without empty droplets. Corrected counts informed the reviewed reference, with raw-reference sensitivity retained. Five residual CM/mural-program fibroblast groups were excluded from the fine reference. Earlier broad-fibroblast median estimated contamination fractions ranged approximately 0.05–0.12 across libraries; these are model estimates, not measured residual contamination after correction. Residual mixing remains possible.

The deposited Xenium matrices contain 403 gene targets plus 138 control/non-gene features. The shared RNA reference contains 381 measured genes. Controls do not enter biological scores. Missing nuclear area alone does not exclude a CM. The earlier multimodal segmentation, transcript reassignment and core/nuclear count layers are retained as sensitivity views, not independent ground truth.

### Fibroblast identities and marker patterns

Identity layers are the newer recommended candidates, the subset supported by cell/core and naming-marker-withheld agreement, and the earlier stringent labels. Broad fibroblast eligibility is frozen. Supported nuclear alternatives remain review flags in the newer labels. Reference classes absent at an age are marked not assessable in the table; their programs can still be measured directly. Candidate counts measure operational coverage rather than tissue abundance. Thresholds selected from reference cross-validation predictions do not provide unbiased prospective spatial accuracy.

Seven measured patterns are analyzed: Postn, Gfpt2, Col8a1 and Lsamp marker sets, plus fibrillar, basement and elastic matrix sets. Exact genes are supplied in the program table and source files. Scores are mean within-cell gene ranks minus the average of 200 control sets matched to pooled clean RNA-reference abundance deciles. The four identity-marker scores reproduce the previous module files. A descriptive enrichment flag exceeds 90% of control sets and requires two detected markers; it is not a cell-type probability or single-cell significance test.

Each source view requires at least 20 shared-panel molecules and eight detected genes. Programs are averaged across all informative eligible fibroblast neighbors, including cells without a confident subtype or enrichment flag. A program is tested only if at least 30 informative fibroblasts detect two of its markers. Identity fractions use all eligible broad fibroblast neighbors, including unassigned cells. Fibroblast program genes do not overlap the CM maturation panel. A separate sensitivity removes CM maturation, lineage/response, cycling and sex genes from both the fibroblast rank background and control pool, then recomputes scores and fits; these excluded genes and retained counts are supplied.

Refined whole-cell, inner-cell core and native deposited counts are compared. Three binomial-thinning repetitions reduce refined counts toward each profile's core depth without changing its geometry; their mean score provides an information-loss control. Core and depth-matched comparisons use the same informative-source mask. These tests do not fully propagate boundary uncertainty.

### Cardiomyocyte outcomes and cohorts

The frozen ventricular cohort retains ventricular-marker-supported profiles with compatible released v.CM, Ankrd1.CM, Slit2.CM, Ddc.CM or p.CM labels and the earlier morphology rule. CMs require at least 20 core-panel molecules and 20 molecules outside the outcome genes. The p.CM label denotes a cycling-associated state, not demonstrated completed cell division.

The measured 21-gene panel is Rxrg, Ppargc1a, Ppargc1b, Cebpa, Cebpb, Esrra, Esrrb, Esrrg, Mitf, Myh6, Tnni3, Myl2, Kcnq1, Jph1, Jph2, Bin1, Lpin1, Pln, Ppara, Ppard and Cpt1b. The frozen direct index is the mean gene-standardized log-normalized expression using the earlier pooled calibration. It is not an exact reproduction of the author's unrecovered AddModuleScore object.

Metabolic-regulatory genes are Ppargc1a, Ppargc1b, Esrra, Esrrg, Ppara, Ppard, Cpt1b and Lpin1. Sarcomere genes are Myh6, Tnni3 and Myl2. Excitation–contraction genes are Pln, Jph1, Jph2, Bin1 and Kcnq1. Cycling uses Mki67, Top2a and Cenpf, with higher values indicating higher cycling-associated RNA. Thus Lpin1 and Pln are grouped by their biological roles rather than reproducing the figure's category placement. Removing Myl2 yields a 20-gene index and a two-gene sarcomere sensitivity without changing ventricular selection. Native CM outcomes provide an additional transcript-ownership sensitivity.

Within each graph, source views use the same eligible CMs. The 50-µm cohorts contain 13,071 P0, 27,659 P7, 26,996 P14 and 27,053 P21 CMs before individual-state model eligibility. Paired 50-/30-µm and shared-boundary comparisons use their common CM cohorts. A fitted state requires at least 200 CMs, eight 400-µm blocks and sufficient residual exposure variation. Categorical exposures also require 50 positive CMs.

### Association models and uncertainty

Primary fibroblast models test one identity fraction or program at a time. They adjust for CM capture outside the outcomes, area, nuclear missingness, neighbor counts, broad non-CM composition and FB/EC capture. Program models additionally adjust for informative-source coverage, depth and detected genes. Anatomical models add arterial/endocardial/large-void/outer-surface distance proxies, morphology class and nuclear-supported capillary/endocardial fractions. The primary comparison removes means within 200-µm regions, separately by CM state. These proxies are not independently reviewed LV/RV or compact/trabecular masks.

Targets and outcomes are standardized within each age/run before separating CM states. Rank-revealing QR removes redundant nuisance columns. Each point estimate is fitted by least squares. Shared 512-resample, 400-µm block bootstraps provide exploratory within-section intervals and CM-subtype slope comparisons. Technical, anatomical and within-region fits are retained separately. Joint-program fits examine specificity; single-program estimates are not assumed independent of other matrix patterns.

Vascular-target models adjust for the revised fibroblast fractions; a sensitivity replaces these fractions with all seven fibroblast programs. The primary family comprises 925 tests across all four ages, eligible CM states, candidate identities, refined programs, two vascular targets and five primary outcomes. One global BH correction is applied to this complete grid. Sensitivity outcomes and alternative definitions are supplied separately and are not used to declare additional corrected discoveries. Different detectability across ages is not treated as a demonstrated age interaction.

### Fibroblast spatial comparisons and temporal descriptions

The P7 Gfpt2/Postn and P14 Col8a1/Gfpt2 comparisons repeat the earlier matching design with the new labels. Profiles need at least five resolved neighbors within 30 µm. Matching uses standardized log RNA molecules, log area, log 50-µm density and nuclear fraction; nearest reference matching permits replacement with a Euclidean caliper of two. Pair IDs, unique reference counts and covariate balance are supplied. Arterial distance is to the earlier operational structure, not a newly traced histological vessel wall. These are descriptive spatial contrasts.

The 96 frozen neighborhoods retain their selected cells, molecular representation, exact transport plans and saved Wormhole plans [3]. Updated fibroblast identities and programs are held-out descriptors. They may share underlying RNA information with the molecular representation, so this is not independent validation. Reported program changes condition on pairs with an informative fibroblast at both ends, and the supported matching mass is supplied. Fine labels do not retrain the encoder. Matching is not ancestry, expansion or a measured transition rate.

### Validation and limitations

Independent checks reconstruct 8,064 neighborhood program means, compare 36 regression coefficients with a separate residualization calculation, verify assignment coverage and unavailable classes, recalculate the full primary BH correction and check design conditioning. Source IDs, source tables, count views and program definitions are retained. Source/artifact hashes and document/figure checks accompany the release.

The atlas's strict outline benchmark deteriorates at P21; its object-overlap score is not transcript-ownership accuracy. Core/native/thinned and shared-boundary checks expose some sensitivities but do not integrate uncertainty over plausible alternative boundaries. Neither computational agreement nor spatial-block intervals establish independent-heart replication. Blood flow, matrix mechanics, functional CM maturation and cell ancestry were not measured here.
